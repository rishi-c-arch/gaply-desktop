Title: Sleep Duration and Memory Consolidation: A Randomized Study

Abstract
We tested whether a full night of sleep improves consolidation of newly
learned material relative to an equivalent period of daytime wakefulness.

Introduction
Sleep is widely believed to support the consolidation of declarative memory.
Prior studies report mixed effect sizes, motivating a preregistered test.

Methods
We recruited 48 healthy adults (aged 18-35) and randomly assigned them to a
sleep or wake condition. Recall was scored the next morning and compared with a
paired t-test.

Results
The sleep group recalled significantly more items than the wake group
(t(47) = 3.2, p = 0.002, d = 0.46), a moderate effect.

Discussion
The findings are consistent with an active-consolidation account of sleep and
align with the larger reported effects in the literature.

Conclusion
A full night of sleep improved memory consolidation in this sample.
