Introduction

Poorly water-soluble active pharmaceutical ingredients remain a central formulation
challenge, and amorphous solid dispersions have emerged as a leading strategy to
improve apparent solubility and oral bioavailability [1]. Spray drying and hot-melt
extrusion are the two dominant manufacturing routes, each with distinct advantages
for thermally labile compounds [1,2]. Polymer selection governs both the physical
stability of the dispersion and the extent of supersaturation maintained in the
gastrointestinal tract [3,5]. Recent work has shown that ternary systems combining a
cellulosic polymer with a surfactant can prolong the supersaturated state well beyond
what either excipient achieves alone [4-6].

Results

Dissolution testing under non-sink conditions showed that the extruded dispersions
maintained supersaturation for at least four hours, whereas the crystalline reference
fell to equilibrium solubility within thirty minutes [7,8]. Powder X-ray diffraction
confirmed the absence of crystalline drug in all freshly prepared batches [9]. After
twelve weeks of accelerated storage, the binary dispersions showed partial
recrystallization, while the ternary formulations remained amorphous [10-12].
